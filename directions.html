📚 2. Core Vocabulary

Go straight ahead Vai dritto

Walk forward in the same direction, without turning.

"Go straight ahead on Pine Street for two blocks."

Turn left / right Gira a sin./destr.

Change direction to the left or to the right side.

"Walk to the corner of Main Street and turn left."

Take the first right Prendi la prima a dx

Turn right at the very first street or intersection you reach.

"Go down Park Road, and take the first right."

Go past Supera / Passa oltre

Walk or drive in front of a landmark without stopping.

"Go past the supermarket. The hospital is next to it."

Opposite Di fronte (strada)

On the other side of the street, facing the location.

"The Post Office is opposite the Library across the street."

At the corner of All'angolo di

The point where two streets intersect or join.

"The Cinema is at the corner of Main Street and High Street."

Off the beaten track Fuori mano / Isolato

Far away from where most people live, travel, or visit.

"She lives in a quiet village that is off the beaten track."

In the middle of nowhere In mezzo al nulla

An isolated place that is very far away from any towns or services.

"Their car broke down in the middle of nowhere, with no signal."

A stone's throw A due passi

A very short distance; very close by.

"The Cafe is just a stone's throw from the Post Office."

As the crow flies In linea d'aria

The shortest direct distance between two points (in a straight line).

"It is only one mile as the crow flies, but much longer by road."

Dead end Strada senza uscita

A road or street that is closed at one end and goes nowhere.

"Be careful not to drive down that lane; it's a dead end."

Fork in the road Bivio / Incrocio a Y

A point where a single road splits into two different directions.

"When you reach the fork in the road, turn left to stay on path."

🗣️ 3. Conversation Scenario: "Looking for the Hospital"

Sarah (Tourist)

Excuse me! Can you help me? I'm trying to find the Hospital. I am staying at a place that is off the beaten track—it honestly feels like it's in the middle of nowhere—and I got completely lost trying to walk into town!

Paolo (Local)

Hello! Ah, yes, some of the countryside accommodations are very isolated. But don't worry, you are in town now, and the Hospital is not far. Actually, it is just a stone's throw from the School, though it is further if you drive.

Sarah (Tourist)

Oh, thank goodness! How do I get there from here?

Paolo (Local)

Go straight down Pine Street heading south. Cross Broadway. Watch out, don't turn down the small side lane on the left—it's a dead end! Keep walking past the School on your left until you reach a fork in the road with Maple Avenue. Turn right there, and the Hospital will be on your right, next to the Bus Station.

Sarah (Tourist)

Ah, ok. So I go down Pine Street, cross Broadway, avoid the dead end, walk past the School, and turn right at the fork onto Maple Avenue. It is just a mile as the crow flies, right?

Paolo (Local)

Yes, exactly! It is opposite the School across Pine Street, but the entrance is on Maple Avenue. You can't miss it.

Sarah (Tourist)

Perfect! Thank you so much for your help!

Paolo (Local)

You're very welcome! Have a nice day!

👄 4. Italian Speaker Pronunciation & Grammar Focus

🇮🇹 ➔ 🇬🇧 Overcoming Common Transfer Mistakes

When Italian speakers give directions, standard grammar and vowel sounds often get mixed. Practice these corrections below during your class call:

1. Prepositions: Stop saying "on the left"

  • ❌ "Turn on the left" ➔ "Turn left"
  • ❌ "Go to the right" ➔ "Turn right"
  • ❌ "Walk in Broadway" ➔ "Walk on Broadway"
  • 💡 Note: In English, "left" and "right" act as adverbs here. No prepositions are needed.

2. Opposite vs. In Front Of

  • ❌ "The library is in front of the supermarket." (meaning across the street)
  • ➔ "The library is opposite the supermarket."
  • 💡 Note: In English, "in front of" means right next to it, facing the same direction. If a street separates them, use "opposite".

3. Silent "H" Aspiration Practice

  • ❌ "You are 'ere." ➔ "You are here." (Pronounce the breathing /h/)
  • ❌ "Go to the 'ospital." ➔ "Go to the hospital."
  • ❌ "It's be-yind the cinema." ➔ "It's behind the cinema."
  • 💡 Practice blow: Place your hand in front of your mouth and feel the puff of air on "here", "hospital", and "behind".

4. Vowel Epenthesis (The "uh" sound)

  • ❌ "Turn left-uh and go past-uh the street-uh."
  • ➔ "Turn left and go past the street."
  • 💡 Practice: End the consonants cleanly. Stop the vocal cord vibration immediately after releasing the /t/ in left, right, and past.

Exercise A: True or False? (Based on Map)

1. The Cafe is next to the Post Office.

Correct: True (They sit side-by-side in Block B)

2. The Hospital is opposite the School across Pine Street.

Correct: True (They are separated by Pine Street in the bottom row)

3. The Museum is next to the Supermarket.

Correct: False (They are separated by Pine Street)

4. The Bus Station is next to the Hospital.

Correct: False (They are separated by Park Road)

5. The Gym is on the corner of Broadway and High Street.

Correct: True (It is on the corner of the intersection of Broadway and High Street)

Exercise B: Tracing Routes (Fill in the Blanks)

1. To get to the Cafe, walk North on Pine Street, then turn ______ onto Main Street. The Cafe will be on your right.

Hint: Walk north and turn towards the west side.
Correct: left

2. To get to the Gym, walk East on Broadway, and turn ______ onto High Street. The Gym will be on your left.

Hint: Walk east and turn towards the south side.
Correct: right

3. The Museum is situated ______ the Supermarket and the Train Station.

Hint: In the middle of these two structures.
Correct: between

4. The Post Office is ______ the Library across Pine Street.

Hint: Facing each other on opposite sides of Pine Street.
Correct: opposite

5. To go to the Bus Station from 'You Are Here', go straight ______ on Broadway. The Bus Station is at the end of the street.

Hint: Move forward along the street (ahead / along / west).
Correct: ahead / along / west
